# The Story of Go30

## Where Go30 Came From

Go30 began in June 2018 within F3 Puget Sound.

According to Dilfer, the idea originated with Dig Dug after attending GTE 4 and learning more about Shield Locks, accountability, and intentional personal growth.

> "He came up with an idea of just making a simple spreadsheet and having guys come up with something that they can do or not do every day of the month and check in on this spreadsheet. Did you do it? Did you not do it? That's it." — Dilfer

The original version was remarkably simple. Participants selected one daily goal, recorded whether they succeeded, and allowed other men to see their progress. Approximately twenty to twenty-five men participated during the first month.

Over time, the process evolved. Teams were formed, bonus opportunities were added, accountability structures became stronger, and the system eventually grew into the Go30 experience that exists today.

---

## What Is Go30?

Go30 is a monthly accountability challenge designed to help men intentionally develop positive habits, eliminate destructive habits, and become the men they aspire to be.

Participants choose a goal to complete (or avoid) every day throughout the month. Every day, they check in and record whether they were successful.

At its core, Go30 is built upon three simple ideas:

* Small actions performed consistently create lasting change.
* Accountability is a force multiplier.
* Men grow more effectively in community than in isolation.

As Dilfer explained:

> "It's so funny, though. It's so simple. You could easily just fake it, but nobody's accelerating when you do that."

---

## Why Thirty Days?

The thirty-day format makes the process approachable.

Many people set enormous goals at the beginning of a year only to abandon them a few weeks later. Go30 intentionally breaks those ambitions into manageable pieces.

Dilfer explained it this way:

> "It's just thirty days. It's not a whole year. It's a smaller bite."

Rather than attempting a dramatic transformation, participants are encouraged to identify one small action and perform it consistently.

---

## The Influence of Atomic Habits

Over the years, Go30 became heavily influenced by the ideas presented in James Clear's book *Atomic Habits*.

Silo described the philosophy this way:

> "We try to come up with the idea of atomic habits—one small incremental thing you can do each day."

The emphasis shifted from achieving enormous outcomes to creating sustainable systems and routines.

Dilfer used the example of someone who wants to run a marathon:

> "You don't start running five miles or ten miles a day. The habit might be to put on your running shoes every day for a month. Eventually you'll build from there."

---

## Examples of Goals

Participants are encouraged to choose goals that are challenging enough to create growth while remaining realistic and sustainable.

Goals have included:

* Daily exercise
* Meditation
* Reading
* Journaling
* Learning a language
* Stretching
* Intermittent fasting
* Limiting social media usage
* Avoiding pornography
* Eliminating sugar
* Reducing screen time
* Avoiding distracted driving
* Spending more intentional time with family

Silo shared his own experience of beginning a daily Duolingo practice before visiting Germany.

> "Here I am almost two years later still keeping that streak up."

Ernie described his experience with intermittent fasting:

> "It's been something I'd be really hot on for a week or two, and then I'd stop. Over the last several months, I've actually been consistently doing it."

---

## How Accountability Works

Go30 works because participants are accountable not only to themselves but also to the other men on their teams.

Each participant records his progress daily. Other members can see successes, failures, and missed check-ins.

Dilfer described the process:

> "Everyone can see if you left it blank. 'Hey, what's going on? That's two days in a row that you missed.'"

As the program evolved, teams were organized according to AOs whenever possible so that participants could see one another regularly throughout the month.

Teams communicate in whatever way works best for them:

* Group text messages
* Slack channels
* Face-to-face meetings
* Regular check-ins
* Coffeeteria

Silo explained that the goal is to create both accountability and proximity.

> "It's not just texting every day and not seeing these people for a whole month. You can sit down and talk about it face to face."

---

## Identity, Not Achievement

One of the recurring themes throughout the discussion was that Go30 is not fundamentally about accomplishing tasks.

It is about becoming someone different.

Ernie explained:

> "Ultimately, it's about creating habits that you don't think about anymore."

He went on to explain that the objective is to reach the point where positive behaviors become as automatic as brushing your teeth every morning.

The question eventually becomes not, "What do I want to accomplish?" but rather, "Who do I want to become?"

---

## The Mental Health Benefit

An unexpected benefit of Go30 has been its effect on the emotional and relational health of participants.

Over time, patterns emerge.

Successes become visible.

Struggles become visible as well.

Dilfer described how the spreadsheet's visual indicators sometimes reveal that a participant is having difficulty long before he asks for help.

> "You can see everybody's progress. It's kind of a nice little barometer."

He continued:

> "There have been several instances where guys have reached out to me after seeing a string of red or black and asked, 'What's going on?'"

The result is deeper accountability, stronger friendships, and better support for men who may be struggling.

---

## The Real Purpose of Go30

Go30 is not a competition, even though friendly competition is certainly part of the experience.

Go30 is not a productivity system.

Go30 is not a spreadsheet.

Go30 exists to help men align their daily actions with the lives they want to lead.

As the hosts of the podcast observed, the greatest changes often begin with the smallest actions.

A daily choice becomes a habit.

A habit becomes part of one's identity.

Over time, a man becomes the person he has been training himself to become.

That is the purpose of Go30.
